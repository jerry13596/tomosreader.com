Privacy policy — Tomos

Last changed: 23 November 2026 · App version: 0.29.0

This text exists in 13 languages with the same content. If two languages differ, whichever protects you more applies.

The short of it

The free version shows Google's ads, and so the app has internet access; the full version shows no ads. What the app keeps about your reading it gives to no ad, and it does not reach us. There is no account and no server of ours, and our own code sends no usage statistics and no crash reports. What goes to the internet is Google's libraries inside the app: in the free version for the ads and for your consent to them, and, in either version, for buying the full version through Google Play; what they send, and when, is below, «What Google's libraries send». Otherwise what you do stays on your phone, and only what you send out leaves it, in one of the ways in the list just below, «When you send something out». A reading you started can, however, be carried on from the lock screen by whoever holds your phone (below, «Reading aloud with the screen off»).

Which permissions the app declares can be seen in its own file, the APK, and a check on the APK as it is built fails if they change. There are exactly ten, all of the kind Android grants at install without asking: FOREGROUND_SERVICE and FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK, for reading aloud with the screen off (below); INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, so that Google's libraries can reach the internet and see whether there is a connection; com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID, for the phone's advertising ID; ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS, for Android's own advertising features, where the phone has them; WAKE_LOCK, so that the phone stays awake while Google's libraries finish some of their work; and com.android.vending.BILLING, for buying through Google Play. The free and the full version are the same file, so the full version has them too. None gives access to your files, photos, contacts or location.

There is one more «permission» line inside the file, written here because anyone who opens it will see it: the app defines and holds it for itself. You are never asked for it, and it gives access to nothing on your phone — a Google library adds it so that an internal message channel stays closed to other apps. It is a lock, not a key.

When you send something out

Every way in which the app gives anything to another app or device is in this list, or in the next one, «What Google's libraries send». An automatic check searches the app's code for such ways, and fails when it finds one that is not written here.

  1. 1. Reading aloud. In the full version only. When you start it — with the «Voice» button on the bottom bar, or, if you hid it from the bar, with «Settings» → «Voice» → «Read aloud from this page»; by holding a word to select it and tapping «Read aloud from here» on the selection bar, or by holding it alone, if you set it so: «Settings» → «Controls» → «Starts reading from there» (while it is already reading, the hold is enough); or from the notification and the lock screen, or with a headset's button, for a reading you began earlier in the book — the text of the page goes to your phone's own voice: the speech engine the phone gives to apps, normally the one set on the phone, usually Google's or Samsung's; on some phones the maker's engine serves only the maker's own apps, and Android gives another, usually Google's. It is another app, with its own internet access. Only voices that it itself reports as working without the internet and already installed are asked for. What that app does with the text cannot be checked from here; its maker decides. The app does not save the speech: it is heard at that moment. Before the first time, the app tells you this and waits for you to agree.
  2. 2. The reading's card, in the notification and on the lock screen, and perhaps on a device you have connected over Bluetooth, such as a car's screen: it shows only «Reading aloud» and the voice. What exactly shows, and who else sees it: below, «Reading aloud with the screen off».
  3. 3. Recommending a book to a friend. When you tap «Recommend to a friend»: the title, the author, your rating and your line, as plain text, to the app you choose yourself from the phone's list. We see neither what you sent nor to whom: the phone does it, not us.
  4. 4. The seven fields you type in: «Why you recommend it», «Go to page», «Search in the book», «Your note», in a note's box, «Name», when you make or change a category, «Shelf title», when you give or change a shelf's title, and «Password», when you lock or open a backup. What you type there is seen by your keyboard, which is another app too, as in any app; it is asked not to learn from these fields, and whether it does is up to it. What you copy or cut from them goes to the phone's clipboard, from where the app you paste it into takes it. The selection menu in these fields has only cut, copy, paste and select all: no button of another app, even if your phone offers some. For «Password» the keyboard is also told that it is a password, so that it neither suggests nor corrects it.
  5. 5. A move to a new phone, if you make one: at the end of «What the app keeps», below.
  6. 6. Sending to an app. When you select text in an EPUB book and tap «Send to an app», or, with a screen reader such as TalkBack, use the action «Send the paragraph to an app» on a paragraph, for as much of the paragraph as the page shows: only that text, as plain text, with no title, author or page, to the app you choose yourself from the phone's list. On Android 8 and later the list always shows; on Android 7, if only one app takes text, that app opens straight away. If none takes it, the phone shows its list empty; if even the list does not open, the app says so. The list is the phone's: the app does not look for which apps you have and does not learn which one you chose. The text is given read-only, and nothing comes back from there. It is another app, not ours: it may send the text over the internet or keep it, and that cannot be checked from here. Before the first send, the app tells you this, once.
  7. 7. Copying. When you tap «Copy» on text you selected in an EPUB book, or, with a screen reader, use the action «Copy the paragraph» on a paragraph, for as much of the paragraph as the page shows: only that text. When you tap «Copy» on a phrase or note in «My phrases», or «Copy all from this book»: the book's words, your own words of a note, and the title, author and place, as the screen shows them, and for all of them the names of the sections too. They go to the phone's clipboard, from where the app you paste them into takes them. Your keyboard sees the clipboard too; some keyboards and phones keep a clipboard history or pass it to your other devices: they decide that.
  8. 8. The backup. When you tap «Make a backup» in «Backup», or «First make a backup of this phone» before a restore: what the app keeps, as the table of «What the app keeps» below says — except the spare and temporary copies, that you agreed to the notice about reading aloud, that you saw the explanation of sending to an app, that you have the full version, how many times the app has opened, which first-time tips you have seen, and what Google's libraries keep by themselves — and, if you choose «With the books», the books' copies with their covers, go into one file. With them go the things needed to read and check it, which the app does not otherwise keep: that it is a backup of the app and the numbers of its layout, the day it was made, the app's version, whether the books are inside, the names of its parts, for each of its three parts with what the app keeps the size, a fingerprint and how many records it holds, for each book the size and a fingerprint of its file, and for each cover inside the size and a fingerprint. The app writes the file where you choose, in the phone's «Save» list: on the phone itself, on a memory card, or in an app that keeps files, and then perhaps on the internet too, by that app and under its own terms. The app sends it nowhere else, hands it to no app to send to anyone, and does not keep where you put it. Without a password the file is not locked: whoever has it, or the app you put it in, can read what is inside, with any program that opens zip files. With a password it is encrypted whole, apart from a small beginning that says it is locked; its size can always be seen. The app keeps the password nowhere, and if you forget it the file does not open. The file stays where you put it after you uninstall the app too; you delete it yourself, from there. With «Restore from a backup» you choose a file yourself from the phone's list, and the app reads only that one.

And what is on the screen is seen, as in any app, by the phone's features that you turn on yourself: an accessibility service, which can also read the text of the page, the phone's assistant when you ask it to look at the screen, a screenshot.

The app never sends a book's file to another app or to anyone; only a move to a new phone and a backup «With the books», which you make yourself, take the books' copies along too; the backup, only where you put it.

What Google's libraries send

Inside the app are three of Google's libraries: for ads (Google Mobile Ads), for consent (User Messaging Platform) and for Google Play's purchases (Google Play Billing), with whatever they bring along. They send to and receive from the internet, under Google's terms. Our own code gives them only what is written here: nothing of your books, phrases, notes or reading.

  1. 9. Ads, in the free version only. The app asks Google for an ad only from the fourth time it opens on: when it opens, and while a book you opened from the library has been open for at least a minute, so as to have one ready for when you close it; if none comes, it asks again two minutes after the previous request, while the book stays open, with at most three requests in the first minutes. An ad that waits is never shown once an hour has passed since it came (the time the phone sleeps counts too): while the book stays open, the app then lets it go and asks for a new one, and whenever a request brings no ad, asks again an hour later; that is, after the first minutes, at most one request an hour. The book stays open also while the app is behind another one or the screen is off. It shows the book's ad only when you close the book to go back to the library, never while you read. With the request the app gives nothing about you or about the book. Google's ads library, however, collects and sends by itself, as Google itself puts it: the phone's IP address, from which your general area may be worked out; what you do in the app and with the ad, such as that the app opened, taps and the videos you watch; information on how the app and the library perform, such as how long the app takes to open, how often it hangs and how much energy it uses; and identifiers of the phone and of accounts: the advertising ID, the «app set ID» and, where it applies, other identifiers related to accounts signed in on the phone (the advertising ID, however, it does not send, since the app asks for the treatment described just below); all of it encrypted in transit. Google uses them for ads, for measurement and to detect fraud. From when an ad is asked for and when one is shown, Google can learn that you opened the app, that you kept a book open for at least a minute and roughly for how long, hour by hour, or that you closed it, not which book. With every request the app asks Google to treat the user as a minor under the age of consent. Then, as Google puts it, the ads are not personalised and not remarketing, no requests go to third-party ad vendors such as measurement tools, and the advertising ID is not sent — whatever you answer in the consent window (see just below). The ad is shown and closed by Google's library; if you tap it, whatever it shows opens, usually the browser or Google Play. How Google uses information from apps that show its ads: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. You delete or reset the advertising ID yourself, in the phone's settings, under Google's «Ads».
  2. 10. Consent, in the free version only. Every time the app opens, it asks Google's consent library whether your consent is needed, as it is in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland. To answer, the library sends Google information about the phone, such as its IP address. When it is needed, Google shows its own window, in your phone's language, where you consent, do not consent, or choose by purpose and by partner. The window also asks about personalised ads; the app never asks for them, whatever you answer (above, 9). Your consent counts for storing and reading information on your phone for the ads, for measuring them and for detecting fraud. At an opening where the opening's ad may be asked for (see 9), once it has put the question, the app also asks the library what it kept from earlier openings: if it kept an answer you gave in Google's window, with which ads may be asked for, the app starts the ads library and asks for the opening's ad at once, without waiting for Google's answer, unless it has already learned that Google asks for a new answer or allows no ads. It shows it only if Google asks for no new answer and still allows ads, or does not answer (below); otherwise it lets it go unseen. In every other case, as when you have never answered or when no answer was needed the last time, the app does not start the ads library or ask for any ad until Google has answered and, when it shows its window, until you have answered. If, however, Google does not answer at an opening (no connection at that moment, an error, or nothing within ten seconds), the app asks the library what it kept and, until it is closed and opened again, asks for ads only with an answer you gave in Google's window, with which ads may be asked for; without such an answer, none. What you choose the library keeps on your phone and sends with every ad request. If you do not consent, Google shows «limited» ads, or none: they do not use personal data to choose the ad, but the IP address is still used, to bring the ad to the phone, and Google may keep information on the phone only to detect fraud. You change or withdraw your consent whenever you like, in «About, privacy and licences» → «Privacy choices», which appears when Google's library asks for it for your country; a withdrawal applies from then on. In the full version the app does not ask for consent; what you had chosen stays on the phone, and the app asks for no more ads.
  3. 11. Buying the full version. Every time the app opens, in the free and the full version, it asks your phone's Google Play whether the Google account it has there has bought the full version: to open it, or to close it if the purchase was cancelled or refunded. In the free version it also asks whenever you come back to the app, and while a payment waits or waits to be confirmed. On a new phone or after a new install, until Google Play answers that you have it, the app opens as the free version and asks the consent library as in 10; the consent window waits up to 10 seconds for Google Play's answer, and is not shown if Google Play says you have it. When you open «Full version», it asks Google Play for the price; when you tap «Buy», for the purchase; when you tap «Restore purchase», for your purchases, and for the price if it had not come. You pay in Google Play's window, with the account and the payment method you have there: the app sees no card and no other payment detail. From Google Play it gets the details of the purchase Google Play gives every app — among them the order number and its time —, looks only at what was bought, whether the payment is pending or complete, whether the purchase has already been confirmed, and the purchase's code, which it hands back to Google Play to confirm it was received, and keeps only that you have the full version (below, «What the app keeps»); it sends them nowhere else. Google's purchase library carries within it a way to send Google reports on how it works. For the payment and for what Google Play keeps, Google's terms and privacy policy apply. What we see: below, «What can reach us».

Reading aloud with the screen off

Reading aloud goes on when the screen goes off or you open another app. For Android to allow this, the app declares two of its permissions for it: FOREGROUND_SERVICE and FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK. They are granted at install, without anyone asking you, and give access to nothing: not the internet, not anything stored on your phone. They only let the voice go on while the app is out of sight. You are not asked for permission to show notifications: Android shows the reading's notification without it.

  • What shows: while it reads, and for a while after a pause, the notification and the lock screen show only «Reading aloud» and the voice, for example «English (United States) · Voice 1». Never the title, the author or the text of the book. The voice does reveal the book's language.
  • Who else sees it: like any notification, it can be seen, and its buttons pressed, by the apps you have yourself given access to your notifications (a watch's, for example). If Android hands the card to a device you have connected over Bluetooth too, such as a car's screen or a headset with a screen, that device shows the same and can stop or restart the reading and change the page, as from the lock screen, with all that this means below. Whether Android hands it over depends on the phone and on the device; we have not tested it yet.
  • Who controls it: whoever holds your phone, even locked, can stop and restart the reading from the lock screen, change the page — and so the place where you stopped — or the voice. What is heard that way is marked as read, as if you had heard it; and if the voice reaches another page, the day counts as a reading day. After ten minutes paused, the card no longer restarts the reading: a press only closes it, and it also goes by itself as soon as Android allows, usually a few minutes later.
  • When it stops by itself: for a call, an alarm or a voice assistant, when another app starts playing music, or when you take the headphones out, so that it does not go on through the speaker. And with the sleep timer, if you set it («Settings» → «Voice» → «Sleep timer»): after as many minutes of reading as you choose or at the end of the page or of the chapter, at the end of a sentence, as the voice divides them; then its card goes from the notification and the lock screen too. The timer is not kept: any pause cancels it, and so does closing the book. It never starts again by itself. Sounds that only ask the others to be quieter, such as most navigation prompts and notification sounds, play over the reading.

What the app does not do

  • It does not unlock protected (DRM) books and does not explain how; a locked book simply does not open here.
  • It has no account of ours and signs you in to none. The full version is bought with the Google account Google Play has on your phone, through Google Play.
  • Of its own accord it shares nothing that our own code keeps, and never a book file but in a backup «With the books», which goes only where you put it: there are buttons that prepare text for you to send — recommending a book, sending to an app and copying — and until you tap one nothing is prepared.
  • It never saves reading aloud as an audio file, and never asks for a voice that the speech engine reports as needing the internet, a «cloud» voice that would send your book to a company. What the engine itself does is said above: it cannot be checked from here.
  • It gives your reading to no one: what it keeps about your reading (where you stopped, which parts you read, on which days, your bookmarks, phrases and notes) stays on your phone, for you, goes into no ad request and does not leave it on its own. In the free version, however, Google's ads library collects by itself what «What Google's libraries send» says; personalised ads the app never asks for (above, 9).
  • It does not keep what you searched for: what you type in the search inside a book, and what it finds, are written nowhere, and are gone when you close the book. As with any jump, only the place your reading reached is kept.
  • In the full version it asks for no ads, does not start the ads library and does not ask for consent. The library is, however, in the same file, and a small part of it, like Android's WorkManager library, starts by itself every time the app opens, as Google sets it.

Removing what it keeps

Remove a book in the app and its copy, its bookmarks, its page table and whatever else the app kept about it are deleted, except its phrases and notes; your own file stays where you keep it. The phrases and notes of a book you remove stay in «My phrases», with its title and author, until you delete them yourself. Before the removal the app says how many bookmarks go with it, and that its phrases and notes stay. If the bookmarks, phrases and notes were not read that time the app opened, the book is not removed, so that its bookmarks are not left behind. A bookmark is removed on its own too, from the book's «Bookmarks», with the button beside it, where «Undo» writes it again, or with the button of the page on show. A phrase or a note is removed with «Delete», in the book or in «My phrases», where «Undo» writes it again until the bar closes, you keep or change something else from it, or you leave; a note also from its box, with «Delete the note». A category is deleted in «Settings» → «Marks» → «Categories»: tap it, then «Delete the category», where «Undo» writes it again until the settings close or you make, change or delete another category. Its phrases and notes stay, as «Phrase» or «Note», and keep only the category's inner name, which no longer points anywhere. When you change a category's name, the old one is deleted from the app's files too. All the phrases and notes of a book you removed are removed together in «My phrases», with «Delete all from this book», along with whatever else of that book is left in their file. Bookmarks are never removed by themselves: if the app comes to read a book's text differently and cannot find a bookmark's place again, it keeps it, «no place». Nor are phrases and notes: one that cannot be found again is kept, with the line «No longer found in the book; kept.» If a repair at the start loses a book from the library, its bookmarks stay in their file without showing anywhere; if it had phrases or notes too, those show in «My phrases», and «Delete all from this book» removes its bookmarks with them; otherwise the app has no button that removes them, and they go when you uninstall it. Neither of the two «Remove the marks» buttons touches bookmarks, phrases or notes. If the file of bookmarks, phrases and notes is damaged so badly that nothing of it can be read, «Delete the damaged file of bookmarks, phrases and notes» removes all of them, of every book and of the books you removed, and your categories, from the file and from the spare copy of the bookmarks, phrases and notes. A book's reading marks can also be removed on their own, inside the book: «Settings» → «Marks» → «Remove this book's marks», or only from the page on show to the end: «Settings» → «Marks» → «Remove the marks from this page on». They are also removed together with the book's place, from the library: a long press on the book → «Start from the beginning», and its bookmarks, phrases and notes stay. A shelf is removed from its ⋮, with «Remove the shelf»; its books go to the shelf above, or the first shelf's to the one below, and the app asks first. An empty shelf goes at once, and «Undo» writes it again until you change another shelf, open another of the app's screens, switch to the covers, remove the last book too, or the app closes. A shelf's title, when you delete or change it, and when you remove the shelf, is deleted from the app's files too, and from the settings' spare copy. The same goes for your rating or your line: when you remove or change it, the old one is deleted from the app's files too; and for a note's words, when you change or clear them, or make the note a phrase. In all these cases they are removed from their file's spare copy as well; when you remove a book, from both, the library's and that of the bookmarks, phrases and notes. The reading days are removed from «My reading» → «Clear reading days», and with them from the settings' spare copy. That you saw the explanation before the first send to an app cannot be removed inside the app; it goes with the app, when you uninstall it. Reading marks the app can no longer read, because the way it reads the text has changed, are removed by themselves the next time it opens. Before every such removal the spare copy is deleted whole, and it is made again once the files are cleaned, usually at once. On a phone without enough free space, the deletion from the files can be delayed until the app is able to rewrite them; the spare copy is then made again the first time the app opens with room for it, and until then there is none. If there is no room even for the removal itself, it does not happen: what was to be removed stays as it was, and only the spare copy is missing, until the first opening with room. Only three removals can stop half-way: removing a book, after some or even all of its bookmarks may have been deleted; the button of a page with more than one bookmark, after some of them may have been deleted; and «Delete all from this book», after some of its phrases and notes may have been deleted. The app says it did not finish, and what was not removed stays: the book, the page's other bookmarks, or the other phrases and notes.

A backup you made is your own file, outside the app: not even uninstalling deletes it; you delete it yourself from where you put it. A restore replaces what the app keeps with what the backup holds. The phone will hold what the backup held when it was made: whatever you deleted since then comes back, and whatever you made since then goes. The app says so before it does it.

That you have the full version is not deleted from inside the app: only Google Play's answer that the account does not have it deletes it, and it goes with an uninstall; the purchase stays with your account and comes back with «Restore purchase». The count of the first openings goes with an uninstall. The names of the first-time tips you have seen are deleted with «Show the tips again», in «About, privacy and licences» (those shown again are kept again), and go with an uninstall. Your answer on consent you change in «Privacy choices», when it appears; it and whatever else Google's libraries keep go with an uninstall, or when you clear the app's data in the phone's settings, which also deletes everything else the app keeps. What they have already sent to Google, Google keeps, under its own terms: https://policies.google.com/privacy.

Uninstall the app and everything goes with it. For all of this you need to ask no one: what the app keeps on your phone, we never have.

Security and how long

What the app keeps sits in its private area, which Android lets no other app read; on phones launched with Android 10 or later, that area is also encrypted by Android itself. The app adds no encryption of its own to what it keeps on the phone, so the best protection is your phone's own lock (PIN, pattern). What it keeps stays for as long as the app is installed, until you delete it. A backup without a password is protected by nothing from whoever has it; with a password the app encrypts it, with AES-256-GCM and a key from your password (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 600,000 rounds), with Android's own cipher. What the ads library sends travels encrypted (TLS), as Google says.
